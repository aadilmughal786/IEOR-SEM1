> > [!tip]- Understand the Problem  
> > At its core, a **problem is just data**, and an **algorithm is just a way to process that data** to get the answer.  
> >  
> > Everything is data → we **process** it → we get the **output**.  
> > That’s it. That’s the flow.  
> 
> ---
> 
> > [!tip]- Understand the Constraint  
> > **But here’s the twist:**  
> >  
> > Problems come with **constraints** — that’s what forces us to **think deeper**, optimize, and find smarter algorithms.  
> >  
> > The real flex is choosing the one that survives the constraints — **fast enough**, **small enough**, or just **clever enough** to get the job done.
> 
> ---
> 
> > [!tip]- Understand the Data Structure  
> > Now, if the **data has structure**, we can process it even **faster and smarter**.  
> >  
> > That’s where **data structures** come in.  
> > The **right data structure** can make a hard problem easy, or an impossible one solvable.  
> >  
> > So it’s not just about writing an algorithm — it’s also about **designing the right structure to shape the data**, so the algorithm can **run better**.
> >

> > [!tip]- Which is Best?  
> > Ok, so the goal is to find the **best algorithm** for a problem.  
> > But how do we **compare algorithms**, even before we implement them?  
> >  
> > That’s where **running time analysis** comes in. It lets us compare algorithms in a way that’s **independent of machine or language**.
> > 
> > **In simple terms:**  
> > - You give the running time function an **input** → it tells you **how many steps** the algorithm will take.  
> > - This gives a **theoretical measure of efficiency**.  
> > - We usually focus on the **highest-degree term** (Big-O), because that dominates for large inputs.
>
> ---
> 
> > [!tip]- Is Running Time Sufficient?  
> > - Running time isn’t the **only factor** — real-world performance also depends on the language, compiler, hardware, etc.  
> > - Don’t stress about that now — **understanding logic and analysis** comes first.
> 
> ---
> 
> > [!tip]- After Implementation Struggle  
> > And finally — **bugs are real**. You’ll hit them.  
> > Debugging is something you **only learn by doing**.  
> > There’s **no fixed rule** to find bugs — just instincts built through repetition.  
> >  
> > Be patient. Read your code like a **story**. Trace variables. Add prints. Think.  
> > The more you debug, the **sharper you get**.
